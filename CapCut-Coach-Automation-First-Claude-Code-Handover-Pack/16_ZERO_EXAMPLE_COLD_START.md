# Zero-Example Cold Start and Continuous Learning

## 1. Default onboarding

The default option is **Start with Coach's editing judgment**. Previous finished videos and CapCut projects are not required.

Alternative option: **Learn from previous videos** for owners who later obtain raw/final pairs.

## 2. What Coach knows on day one

Cold-start decisions combine:

- explicit script structure and recording instructions;
- transparent format-playbook rules;
- local audio/visual quality and semantic features;
- Claude's constrained editorial planning over supplied ids/facts;
- platform-safe defaults for aspect ratio, duration, text readability and pacing;
- brand/asset rules supplied for the current job.

Do not call this the owner's personal style until enough owner evidence exists.

## 3. Cold-start profile

Initial neutral defaults for short vertical videos:

- establish the promise/context in the first 1–2 seconds;
- prefer a visually distinct opening;
- preserve complete factual meaning;
- avoid adjacent near-duplicate angles;
- use clean cuts by default and effects only with purpose;
- keep text inside safe zones and readable at phone size;
- show proof/result before CTA;
- one clear CTA;
- music optional and not required for plan quality;
- generate more than one candidate rather than pretending there is one correct edit.

These are editable defaults, not hard-coded universal truths.

## 4. Candidate generation

- **Clean** — conservative professional baseline.
- **Enhanced** — default; adds a few high-confidence Coach ideas.
- **Bold** — changes hook/sequence/pattern interrupts while preserving facts.

Each meaningful difference is recorded as a decision dimension so feedback is learnable.

## 5. Learning from use without CapCut projects

Collect signals from:

- chosen candidate;
- clip replacements;
- reordered or restored shots;
- text edits;
- pace controls;
- accepted/rejected Coach ideas;
- explicit **Loved it**, **Good** or **Needs work** rating;
- final MP4 imported/detected after optional CapCut changes;
- alignment of that final against Coach's proposal and original raw ranges.

The final MP4 can reveal retained, removed, shortened, reordered, recropped and graphically changed regions even when the editable CapCut timeline is unavailable.

## 6. Preference maturity

Use evidence states rather than pretending accuracy:

- `COLD_START` — 0 approved jobs;
- `EARLY_TASTE` — 1–4 approved jobs;
- `USEFUL_PROFILE` — 5–9 approved jobs;
- `PERSONALISED` — 10–19 approved jobs;
- `MATURE` — 20+ diverse approved jobs with stable evaluation.

The UI shows this as simple language: **Getting to know you**, **Learning quickly**, **Knows your style well**.

## 7. Update policy

- Every completed job creates a candidate preference update.
- Apply low-risk incremental weights automatically only after explicit onboarding consent.
- Large changes, contradictions or a new format require a readable approval card.
- One unusual video never overwrites the main profile.
- Maintain rollback and per-format context.
- Brand assets/claims remain separate from universal taste.

## 8. Active learning

Ask questions only when the answer will materially improve future decisions. Prefer visual A/B questions:

- Which opening?
- Faster or calmer?
- Clean or more dynamic?
- Keep this Coach idea?

Never ask the owner to label embeddings, timecodes or model concepts.

## 9. Expected automation trajectory

For consistent, well-shot scripted short reels:

- cold start: target 45–65% of the complete workflow;
- after five accepted jobs: target 60–75%;
- after 10–20 accepted jobs: target 70–85%;
- repeatable templates with reliable footage may exceed this for the first draft.

These are planning targets and must be measured through active-time studies. They are not quality or performance guarantees.

## 10. Evaluation without historical examples

Use the first three projects as calibration, not marketing evidence.

For projects 4–10 record:

- active owner minutes;
- number of ambiguity questions;
- candidate selected;
- proposal duration retained;
- number of clip/text/structure corrections;
- accepted/rejected Coach ideas;
- export QC issues.

Promotion to Autopilot requires five consecutive completed jobs with no safety failure, no factual correction caused by Coach and owner-rated Good/Loved outcomes.
